# User interviews

These aren’t transcripts from a CRM. They’re **synthetic but grounded** notes—composite conversations from talking to people while building SpendScope. Initials only; companies blurred on purpose.

---

## Interview A — “M.K.” — Founder, 8-person SaaS

CEO who still codes. Bootstrapped. Paying for ChatGPT Team, Cursor, Copilot because “the team asked.”

**What they actually said (paraphrased)**

- “I *know* we’re wasting something. Two hundred or two thousand—I literally can’t tell.”
- “If you say ‘optimize AI spend’ I’m gone. If you tie a number to a price page I’ll at least read it.”
- “Half our Copilot seats are people who still ask how to turn suggestions on. I’m not proud of that.”

**Weird takeaway:** They cared less about the headline savings than about **one sentence that didn’t make them sound stupid** in front of their cofounder.

**What we changed:** Toned down the AI summary when savings were small, and tried to make share pages feel more like a **report** and less like an internal tool screenshot.

---

## Interview B — “R.” — Eng manager, ~25 people

Owns tool choices for eng. Seed-ish company; finance started saying “runway” in meetings.

**Paraphrased quotes**

- “OpenAI’s invoice is a black box. Total at the bottom. No story.”
- “You called us moderate overspend but we’re… enterprise-ish? Is that even the same game?”
- “I don’t want another login. I want something I can PDF and forward.”

**Weird takeaway:** The skepticism wasn’t “your math is wrong” on day one—it was **“does list price even apply to us?”** They wanted the UI to scream **directional** louder than we did.

**What we changed:** More explicit disclaimers on results and in email copy. Stopped implying we had invoice-level truth.

---

## Interview C — “L.” — Indie hacker

Solo + contractors. Sub-$5k MRR. Subscriptions everywhere.

**Paraphrased quotes**

- “I’ll try anything free. You’re not touching my bank.”
- “Savings felt high until I clocked you meant vs *list*, not vs what I actually pay.”
- “Okay the share link is kinda funny—I posted it as a joke and people clicked.”

**Contradiction:** Swore they hated “dashboards,” then spent ten minutes **dragging seat counts** to see the label flip. They wanted a toy; they got a spreadsheet wearing lipstick.

**What we changed:** Kept hammering “benchmarks vs what you typed” in copy so we don’t pretend we’re Plaid.

---

## Interview D — “S.” — Ops / “everything else,” 12 people

Not a CFO. Catches what engineering throws over the wall.

**Paraphrased quotes**

- “Engineering sends screenshots. I need those screenshots to point at a real vendor page.”
- “If AI invented the savings number I’m not taking this to the CEO.”
- “Two tools had basically the same blurb. Felt copy-pasted.”

**Weird takeaway:** They wanted **per-tool differentiation** in the narrative, not one big aggregate number.

**What we changed:** Tightened prompts so Gemini **sticks to engine bullets** instead of freelancing parallel stories per tool.

---

## Interview E — “J.” — Staff IC, bigger company

Curiosity click. Not really the ICP.

**Paraphrased quotes**

- “Neat class-project energy.”
- “I wouldn’t trust savings claims without SSO and real usage data.”
- “…I’d still use it to win an argument in a meeting though.”

**What we changed:** Clarified we’re an **MVP for small teams without finance ops**, not an ITAM replacement. Stopped fantasizing about enterprise gates for v1.

---

## What stuck

People disagreed on **how deep** the product had to go. Almost everyone agreed: **don’t hallucinate money.** And they kept coming back to **“can I forward this?”** So we biased toward **boring benchmarks + careful language** instead of louder AI.
