# User interviews

---

## Interview A — “A.R.” — Student Developer Team, ~10 Members

College project team building multiple AI-assisted projects together. Most members were using different tools individually — ChatGPT, Gemini, Cursor, and Copilot — with no real tracking of who was paying for what.

**What they actually said (paraphrased)**

* “Everybody in the team uses something different, but nobody knows the total monthly cost.”
* “We kept buying tools because one person said it was useful, then the whole team copied it.”
* “Some people literally stopped using Cursor after two weeks but still forgot to cancel it.”
* “The hard part isn’t paying. It’s figuring out whether we’re actually getting value from all these subscriptions.”

**Weird takeaway:** They were less interested in “cutting costs” and more interested in getting a simple overview of the team’s AI stack in one place.

**What we changed:** Added clearer benchmark explanations and simplified recommendation wording so the reports felt understandable even for non-technical users. We also improved the shareable report layout because teams wanted to discuss results together instead of reading raw dashboard data.

## Interview B — “S.P.” — Assistant Professor, Department Research Team (~10 Members)

Faculty-led academic project team using AI tools for research assistance, documentation, coding support, and presentation preparation. Different members were independently subscribing to ChatGPT, Gemini, and GitHub Copilot without centralized tracking.

**What they actually said (paraphrased)**

* “Every student keeps saying they need a different AI tool for productivity, but nobody checks whether the subscriptions overlap.”
* “We started with one ChatGPT subscription and somehow ended up paying for four different AI products across the team.”
* “I don’t mind spending if the tools genuinely save time, but right now the costs feel scattered and hard to justify.”
* “Most dashboards only show usage. They don’t tell me whether we’re overspending.”

**Weird takeaway:** The professor cared less about the exact savings amount and more about having something structured enough to discuss during project reviews and budget approvals.

**What we changed:** Improved the professionalism of the audit summaries and benchmark explanations so the report felt more like a legitimate review document instead of a developer-only dashboard. We also made the recommendations more balanced and less aggressive when the projected savings were small.
