# GTM

Writing this so I don’t pretend the “strategy” is fancier than it is.

## Exact Target User

The person I picture first isn’t “all startups.” It’s usually an **engineering manager or tech lead at a 5–30 person shop**—often pre-Series A—who gets stuck owning the “we’re spending a lot on AI tools” conversation even though nobody hired finance ops yet. They’re in **Cursor, ChatGPT, Copilot, and some API bill** every week. They’re not hunting for procurement software. They want something they can **drop in Slack** or forward before a renewal conversation without feeling like they made it up.

On the side, **bootstrapped founders** who personally pay for a pile of overlapping $20/mo tools fit too. Same itch: “am I dumb for paying for all of this?” They just won’t sit through a demo for a “spend platform.”

## Trigger Moments

Nobody wakes up wanting “AI spend software.” Something cracks: a fat **API invoice**, **seat sprawl** after hires, **duplicate assistants** nobody wants to kill, or a **cost-cutting week** where AI line items are the only knobs that feel movable.

The search is usually **“are we overpaying for Cursor / ChatGPT / Copilot?”**—screenshot attached.

## Where They Hang Out

Not starting with SEO.

**Indie Hackers**, **r/startups / r/SideProject**—people admit what they pay. **HN** if the pitch is methodology, not hype. **X** for short posts with real deltas (anonymized is fine). **Discords** only if you’re actually helpful in billing threads, not spamming links. Tight alumni Slack is gold if you have it; if not, skip pretending.

## First 100 Users Strategy

Week one is supposed to feel a little embarrassing. That’s the point.

I’d ship maybe **five posts** in the “wait, are we overpaying for X?” shape—real numbers from the engine, anonymized, one tool at a time where people can sanity-check seat math themselves. I’d offer **ten free audits** in two communities with a boring CTA: reply with stack + rough monthly spend, I’ll DM something readable. No automation—you learn where the form confuses people.

**Share links** are the loop I care about. If the copy at the end doesn’t feel gross, maybe you get **~15 URLs** floating around even when traffic is tiny. That’s still distribution you can point at.

I’d also DM maybe **twenty people I actually know** and offer a short call where I run the audit live. You get language for the landing page and sometimes one referral. **Show HN** only when the report feels boringly reliable—deterministic numbers, honest disclaimer, one good screenshot. Not a launch party.

If nothing “converts,” you still learned something. You spent time in comments, not on ads nobody clicked.

## Unfair Distribution Advantage

SpendScope isn’t going to win a feature checklist against real spend products.

What it *does* have is a few boring advantages:

**Benchmark visibility** — list math is unsexy, but it’s hard to dismiss in a thread when someone can open the vendor pricing page in another tab.

**Audit virality** — a public `/audit/[id]` link is basically a **forwardable artifact**. No install, no account wall in the happy path.

**No billing OAuth in v1** — yes, that limits depth. It also means “five minutes, no connector drama,” which spreads in corners of the internet that hate enterprise onboarding.

If someday there’s a credits or partner angle, fine—that’s act two. Not claiming it today.

## Week-1 Success Metrics

I’m not optimizing for pageviews.

| Metric | Target | Why I care |
| ------ | ------ | ---------- |
| Completed audits | **40–80** | Did real humans finish without rage-quitting? |
| Share links opened by someone who isn’t the creator | **10–20** | Any sign of pass-along without buying ads? |
| Emails sent from real runs | **5–15/day** when I’m actually posting; **zero** on a dead day is normal |
| Random “can we talk?” / cal clicks | **2–5** | Tiny pipeline beyond “cool demo” |

If people finish audits but never share, I’d look at **whether the report feels embarrassing to forward**—headline, layout, tone—not at throwing more traffic at the top.
