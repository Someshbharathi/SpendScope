# Economics

None of this is a forecast. It’s me scribbling on the back of a napkin so I don’t lie to myself about what a **student-built MVP** with a free audit could theoretically support.

## Lead Value Estimation

Picture the next paid step as something small and human: a **60-minute stack review** or a “help me prep for renewal” package—not a self-serve enterprise contract.

Early on, if you charged like a normal independent, you might land around **$150–250/hr**. Of people who actually finish an audit, maybe **2–5%** book a call within 90 days *if you actually follow up* (most people won’t). If a booked call is roughly **$200** gross and **3%** of completers book, the expected gross per completed audit is on the order of **six bucks** before any costs. That’s tiny on purpose. It’s a reminder that you either need **volume** or a **different wedge later** (rev share, bundles, whatever)—not that you’re printing money on day one.

If you never charge for anything, lead value is **zero**. Still worth saying out loud.

## CAC by Channel (Zero-Budget Assumptions)

When I say CAC here, I mostly mean **founder hours burned per signup**, not ad spend—because there is no ad spend.

| Channel | Cash | What it actually costs |
| ------- | ---- | ------------------------ |
| IH / Reddit | $0 | A few hours per post that doesn’t flop |
| X | $0 | Wild variance; one good thread beats ten mid ones |
| Share-link referrals | ~$0 | Mostly product polish |
| Product Hunt | $0 | A day or two of prep; spike traffic, often meh intent |
| Hacker News | $0 | You might get roasted; if it works, it’s credibility |

The “blended CAC” I’d actually watch is dumb but useful: **how many minutes of support per 10 audits**. Drive that down with better UX, not with more ads.

## Conversion Funnel

Round numbers, don’t treat as truth:

Say **100** people finish an audit. **Three** book a consult (**3%**). **Two** of those pay for a small $300 thing (**two-thirds** of calls). That’s **2%** audit → paid in toy land.

In real life, without nudges, paid conversion might sit **under 1%**. The funnel is sensitive to how aggressively you ask and whether the PDF feels **legit enough to attach to an email**.

The audit is easy to try. The consult needs some trust. Paying you to influence real vendor spend needs more than benchmarks always give you.

## Profitability Logic

A free audit only works if you’re not secretly running a consulting business inside every session.

Infra + Gemini + email needs to stay **absurdly cheap per audit** at MVP volume, and your **time per audit** needs to trend toward zero. If that toy $6 expected gross per audit is in the ballpark, your variable stack cost per audit should be **pennies or less** at small scale—caching summaries, not letting people spam regenerations, that kind of boring stuff.

What actually kills you isn’t hosting. It’s **Slack DMs** and “can you just tweak this in a spreadsheet for me?” That’s CAC in hours.

## Path to $1M ARR (Sanity Check Only)

$1M ARR is roughly **$83k MRR**. I’m not planning my semester around that.

If average customer paid **$400/year**, you’d need thousands of them—doesn’t match this MVP. If you sold something closer to **$6k ARR** per small team, you still need **~167** of those, which is a lot of relationship work. Another path is audit-as-lead-gen: if only **5%** of audits are “qualified” and each turns into a **$2k** project twice a year, you need a **stack** of qualified audits every month—that’s reputation over years, not a weekend launch.

So the honest takeaway for me: **repeat audits + one real monetization wedge first.** The million-dollar story can wait until something actually repeats.
